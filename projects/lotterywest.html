Lotterywest / User research · Journey design · Usability testing

Understanding why customers leave at checkout.

A drop in purchases near draw close prompted a closer look at the payment experience. I worked with a product team to understand why customers were leaving and test ways to make the flow clearer.

ROLE

UX Designer

WHEN

Eight-week sprint

FOCUS

User research · Journey design · Usability testing

IN THIS CASE STUDY

DiscoverExplore & testRefine & deliver

01 / Discover

Look beyond the drop-off.

I interviewed five existing online customers who had left the purchase flow shortly before draw close. We explored their last purchase attempt, motivations, pain points and suggestions.

Task-based observation helped us understand the experience in practice. Re-entering card details emerged as a recurring source of friction.

02 / Explore & test

Test the journey before the final screens.

I reviewed payment patterns across a direct competitor and other retail experiences, then used sketches and concept tests to explore a saved-card feature.

The research considered adding, deleting and changing a primary card, saving during a purchase and handling errors. Tests also surfaced questions about security, confirmation and expired cards.

03 / Refine & deliver

Design around the uncertainty.

The design made card status and action confirmation more visible. Expired cards remained visible so customers could choose to delete them, rather than losing them without explanation.

I used the existing design system and worked with developers and internal stakeholders to refine and validate the interface. Time constraints limited later testing, making further A/B testing a useful next step.
