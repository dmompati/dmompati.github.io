Racing WA / TABTouch / Service design · Facilitation · UX leadership

Bringing clarity to a complex sports experience.

At Racing WA, I combined service blueprinting, retail journey mapping and collaborative workshops with hands-on UX leadership. The work connected customer needs, team priorities and the practical demands of delivering a redesigned sports experience.

ROLE

UX Designer / Acting Product Design Lead

WHEN

May 2024 — Jan 2025

FOCUS

Service design · Facilitation · UX leadership

IN THIS CASE STUDY

01 / The challenge

A redesign in need of a shared direction.

The sports experience spanned mobile, desktop and retail. When I joined, the redesign had been running for nine months and needed clearer UX direction, consistent ways of working and a path to delivery.

The challenge was to modernise the experience while balancing the needs of existing customers with expectations from new audiences. That required understanding the service around the screens as well as the screens themselves.

02 / Understand & map

Make the experience visible.

I developed service blueprints and mapped retail customer journeys, drawing on research and usability evidence to identify where the experience could improve.

A UX audit helped surface inconsistencies in the existing design work. Research and testing explored customer habits, motivations and the clarity of market navigation. Together, these activities gave the team a shared basis for prioritising the work.

03 / Facilitate & align

Bring the right perspectives into the room.

I facilitated cross-functional workshops to realign product goals and establish shared success criteria. This work created space for product, engineering and design to work through priorities together.

I introduced a clearer UX delivery roadmap and aligned design activities with engineering capacity. I also managed three designers, providing mentorship, planning and quality oversight.

Presenting evidence, priorities and trade-offs to senior stakeholders, executives and external providers helped keep decisions connected to the user experience and delivery constraints.

Design leadership

Three designers, with a shared direction across design, product and engineering.

04 / Design & validate

Carry insight through to the experience.

I combined feature-level discovery with prototyping and usability testing, using evidence to challenge assumptions before development.

The design work addressed navigation, market presentation and consistency across the sports experience. Testing and iteration helped the team refine the solution before investing in development.

05 / Outcomes & reflection

A clearer experience. A more aligned team.

The work produced an end-to-end design for the revitalised sports experience and supported its progressive rollout across mobile, desktop and retail. Customer feedback and product insights informed subsequent priorities.

The team gained more structure, visibility and alignment across design, product and engineering. Mentoring and shared design standards supported quality beyond individual features.

My key learning: service improvement needs both a clear view of the customer journey and a practical way for the team to act on it.
